Abstract

In Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs), a well-constrained projector output does not by itself prevent representation collapse. We find that, from initialization, the encoder representation is dominated by a component shared by different inputs. A batch-normalized projector removes this component, so regularization after the projector cannot correct it, and the differences between representations shrink until they collapse. We propose SADL (Supply And Demand Loss), which keeps the original training objective and constrains the encoder directly when needed. SADL compares the representation differences that the projector output demands with the differences the encoder can retain and pass on to the projector, and adds a constraint on the encoder only when supply falls short of demand. It works with different Demand Regularizers and introduces no auxiliary networks, additional trainable parameters, or architectural changes. In single-task and jointly trained four-task LeWorldModel (LeWM) and INTACT experiments, SADL lowers the collapse rate over ten random seeds from 50% to 0%. After one epoch of training, encoder effective rank is 3.4–6.4× that of the corresponding baseline without SADL, and latent prediction error falls to 1/20–1/303 of it. Without additional tuning, SADL stays robust as its loss weight spans orders of magnitude, and with several Demand Regularizers INTACT exceeds 95% mean search-free control success. On four image datasets, SADL also consistently improves accuracy and stability across random seeds.

02 · The blind spot

A well-regularized output cannot see what the encoder has lost.

At initialization the encoder representation H is almost entirely one vector shared by every input: sample variation carries about 0.01% of its energy. A batch-normalized projector subtracts the batch mean, so moving all of H along that shared direction leaves Z unchanged. The Demand Regularizer and the task loss, both computed from Z, get zero gradient there.

03 · Method

Read the demand at the output. Repair the supply at the encoder.

SADL keeps the task loss and the Demand Regularizer unchanged. It reads a detached demand capacity q from Z, measures the supply capacity k of H, and adds a Supply Regularizer on the encoder only when k < q.

01 · Demand capacity

What the output asks for

qreg rises as the Demand Regularizer is satisfied; qexc measures current sample variation at Z. Both are read with stop-gradient.

q=qregqexc

02 · Supply capacity

What the encoder can give

kexc scores the energy and spread of sample differences in H; ktr measures how much reaches Z through regularized canonical correlations.

k = min{kexc, ktr}

03 · One-sided Supply Regularizer

Act only on the shortfall

The penalty grows with the supply deficit and is exactly zero once supply meets demand, so SADL stays out of the way of a healthy encoder.

S = [log(q+ε) − log(k+ε)]+2

Training objective

ℒ = ℒtask + λD D(Z) + λS S(H, sg Z)

06 · Beyond control

The same loss speeds up image representation learning.

On ImageNet-LT, Galaxy10, CIFAR-10 and Imagenette, under matched budgets and Demand Regularizers, SADL improves early learning on all four datasets. Imagenette gains 8.91 points at epoch 20; the gap narrows to 0.44 points by epoch 800.
